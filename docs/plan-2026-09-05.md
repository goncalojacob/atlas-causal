## Decisions taken

1. **Offices are records, not code.** A new kind `office`: owned by an
   actor (`of`), a title, `when`, and a `category` from a small closed set
   — `head-of-state`, `head-of-government`, `legislature`, `party-leadership`,
   `military-command`, `religious`, `other` — so a sultanate, a doge, a
   party's general secretary or a chief are offices of their own actor,
   added by writing a record. Portugal starts with three: monarch,
   president, prime minister.
2. **A tenure is a record kind of its own, `tenure`** (review finding 1:
   a relation's id is from--to--type, so one person could lead one actor
   only once, and Soares held three tenures). Fields: `person`, `office`,
   `when`, optional `startedBy` (the election, coup or succession that
   began it), sources, the envelope. A tenure is always **of the state**
   (`office.of` is `portugal`), never of the regime polity of the moment;
   the regime's own `regime-of` relation carries the regime. Two tenures
   of one office may overlap: a year is the finest bound the model has
   and a regency is not a mistake. **The `led` relation type is retired**
   (owner, 5 September): its twelve records become tenures of a party-
   leadership or head-of-government office, and rule 19 loses the type.
   One strip per office on the state's card, holders as bars over time,
   clustered in one dimension with `cluster.js` past what fits; an
   Offices grouping on the timeline, pre-joined in the topology
   (`officesByEvent`), tenure bars with the holder's events as ticks.
3. **The `degree-zero` warning counts `startedBy`:** an event named by an
   active tenure's `startedBy` is connected, edge or not; the editorial
   bar stays "one honest edge or a tenure it started, never an invented
   edge". The retracted elections that produced a holder come back
   through it (status active, the retraction text cleared from
   `review.note`, the docs file kept as the audit trail, referenced
   actors and places re-checked); elections that changed nobody stay
   retracted. `startedBy` resolves under rule 3, may not name a
   non-active event (rule 11), and warns when the event lies outside the
   tenure's `when`.
4. **Events inside events:** a `parent` field on the event, one parent,
   a tree, no cycles (rule 24); the parent carries its own dates, and a
   child whose `span()` is not inside its parent's is a **warning**, as
   `actor-outside-when` is. **`parent` never enters the adjacency**
   (owner, 5 September): consequences, ancestors, convergence and the
   horizon stay edge-only, and `?chain=` stays a list of edge ids. The
   parent is a display fact: the card says "Part of"; a parent lists its
   parts in order; the timeline draws a parent as a bracket over its
   children; the graph collapses children into their parent when zoomed
   out — semantic collapse runs first and produces the node set, M25's
   geometric stacking runs on that set, and a collapsed parent expands
   under the same never-hide rule M25 uses for the selection; the
   collapsed node's weight is a derived `subtreeWeight` in the topology,
   never a change to `weight`. **Large events look different** (owner):
   an event whose children span more than one lane, or that is flagged
   `scope: regional|worldwide`, is drawn as a band across the whole
   timeline and as a wash over the map rather than a mark, so a world
   war or a pandemic reads as the ground the smaller events stand on.
   **Filter by parent** (owner): the lens gains `event:<id>` — the atlas
   narrows to that event's subtree in every view, with the parent's own
   band kept; the card offers "Show only this" on any parent.
5. **`place` is already optional** (schema and rule 10 since M9; 229
   events carry `place: null`). What is new: the map's corner counts the
   unplaced events in the window, and **`region` becomes optional
   everywhere** (owner, 5 September): rule 10 stops requiring it on a
   placeless event, the form stops calling it "Timeline lane", and the
   region filter skips events that have none. `data/geo/regions.json`
   and `deriveRegion` stay, because the index derives a region for every
   placed event. A polygon place is a later, second kind of place.
6. **Place names are dated through a sibling field.** `names` stays
   `[string]` (search, rule 18, the topology and the i18n overlay all
   depend on that shape); a place gains optional `historicalNames:
   [{ name, from, to }]`, read by the base-map label layer and the place
   card, so a city is labelled by the year shown (Lourenço Marques until
   1976, Maputo after) once somebody has written it.
7. **Roles are a closed list, in data.** `data/roles.json` holds the
   list; `tools/lib/read.mjs` reads it as it reads `data/regions.json`,
   `buildTopology` carries it as `rolesAllowed` (distinct from
   `manifest.roles`, the roles in use), and `checkRules` enforces it from
   the topology so the browser's validator needs no extra fetch; adding
   a role is an edit to that file, never a code change. Each actor line
   on an event becomes `{ actor, role, note }`, the note free text for
   the phrase the drafting runs wrote, carried in the topology (the actor
   card shows it) and listed in the i18n overlay as an index-addressed
   translatable field. The mapping from the 163 roles in
   use to the list is written first, as a table the owner approves, and
   applied by a run afterwards. Starting list (27):
   leader, head-of-state, head-of-government, minister, commander,
   government, opposition, party, candidate, elected, deposed, resigned,
   belligerent, invader, occupier, victim, perpetrator, target,
   signatory, negotiator, founder, member, applicant, host,
   author, movement, institution.
8. **The region grouping goes; `office` comes.** The timeline already
   opens as one packed lane (M14); what changes is that `region` leaves
   `GROUPS` (duplicated in `state.js` and `lanes.js`) and `lanesFor`,
   `office` joins them, and `region` joins the lens vocabulary in
   `FOCUS` and `lens.js` so a reader can still narrow to a continent. The
   continent-assignment question stays open in the backlog; nothing
   depends on it any more.
9. **Base map** (owner's next big project), all Natural Earth 10 m,
   public domain, fetched from the `nvkelso/natural-earth-vector`
   repository on GitHub **by a GitHub Action on an `import/**` branch**
   (a sandbox run has no network; the tool and its pure halves are
   tested offline on fixtures, as the Wikidata import is), sharded by
   zoom level and region — our own vector files from our own origin in
   our own projection, which `ARCHITECTURE.md` records as the boundary of
   the "no map tiles" rule, not an exception to it (owner, 5 September).
   First view under 1 MB, **15 MB ceiling on disk** as a per-layer,
   per-zoom output budget the tool prints before writing, with an order
   of sacrifice when blown (peaks, rivers, lakes, smaller cities,
   physical-region polygons), to revisit after testing:
   coastlines and land at 10 m, rivers, lakes, physical regions (mountain
   ranges, deserts, plains, peninsulas) as polygons with labels at full
   detail, peaks, and cities with population above 100 000 **plus every
   city the atlas holds a place record for**. Labels: modern name and
   local-language name from Natural Earth, dated names from the place
   record when one exists; which place record a Natural Earth city is is
   **data, not code** (`data/imports/naturalearth-places.json`, matched
   on `wikidata` first, candidates reported for the rest, never
   guessed); one label placer shared with the events layer, event labels
   beating city labels beating region labels. Never roads, railways or
   modern regions.
10. Three code-only items stay as they are in the backlog: one renderer
    for the contribution form and the review editor; a record's history
    on the dashboard; a presence's capital as a reference to a place.

12. **A state can be a member.** M29 found that rule 19 puts a *person* at
    the `from` end of `member-of`, so Portugal's ten memberships were
    recorded as `allied-with` with a note. M30a widens rule 19 so a
    polity or an institution may be `member-of` an institution, and M31
    re-types those ten relations; `allied-with` goes back to meaning an
    alliance.

11. **Answering "why".** A reader arrives with a question, not an event:
    *why is Angola poor?* The atlas answers questions like that only
    through its endpoints, so two things are needed and both are built
    here. (a) An **endpoint can be a condition**: the schema already
    allows a process without a point; the brief says so plainly and the
    drafting guidance names conditions ("Angolan economy dependent on
    oil, 1975–") as events worth writing, with the same sources and
    edges as any other. (b) A **"Why" mode** on any event, actor or
    place: the ancestors of the endpoint (for an actor or a place, of its
    events in the window), laid out by depth in the graph view with the
    strongest paths by confidence and weight emphasised, and beside it
    the **path read as prose** — the edge explanations along the chosen
    path stitched in order, each with its confidence and dispute marks,
    which is what the explanations exist for and what an on-demand
    narrative will later be built from. Entry: a "Why?" control on the
    card, the search box accepting a question word ("why angola") and
    offering the actor's endpoints, and `?why=<id>` in the URL. The
    convergence query is untouched.
