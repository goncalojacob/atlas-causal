What this is

The history of the world since 1492 as a graph: every event linked to what caused it and what it led to, with sources. A map, a timeline and a graph over the same records — events as nodes, the places they happened at, the people, polities and institutions in them as actors, the ground those actors held as territories, and causal links as edges. Each edge carries a type, a confidence level and a written argument with its sources.

Pick an event, follow its consequences, and see which other branches fed the same endpoint — the antidote to reading one chain as the explanation.

Who makes it, and what has been read

One person, with an assistant: most of the records here are drafted from Wikipedia and Wikidata, and each of those credits on its card the article and the revision it was read at. Every causal link carries a written argument and at least one source, and the card says how sure the atlas is of it. A person is reading the drafts one at a time, and adding ?review=1 to any address shows each record's review state on the page, as the person reading them sees it.

The territories and many of the polities are imports, not writing: they are read off somebody else's dataset and carry that dataset's own licence and its own caveats. An import never signs anything and never rewrites a record a person has signed.

How sure the atlas is

Confidence is defined by evidence, so that it can be checked rather than felt. Where historians disagree, the atlas says so and says who, instead of picking a side.

consensus
The link is accepted. It requires at least two cited sources by different authors — a necessary condition, not a sufficient one: a reviewer still judges whether they really agree.
probable
Supported by the sources cited, with no known dissent. Most edges live here, and that is honest: it says the argument rests on the sources given and nobody has been found arguing the other way.
disputed
Qualified historians disagree about the link itself — whether it exists, what type it is, or how much it carries. The card names who disagrees.

Licences

Four, because one cannot honestly cover code, prose and two imported datasets.

  • The code is MIT.
  • The records — everything a person wrote — are CC BY-SA 4.0.
  • The coastlines derive from Natural Earth, which is public domain and asks to be credited.
  • The territories are under neither of those. From 1886 they are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, because CShapes 2.0 is, and that licence forbids commercial use; before 1886 they are GPL-3.0, because Historical Basemaps is. They are kept in their own directories, every file says which licence it carries, and nothing is ever copied out of them into a record somebody wrote.

Relicensing stops being possible the moment a stranger's paragraph is merged, so the licence is a decision taken once rather than a default to revisit. The full attributions are in data/LICENSE and data/geo/LICENSE, and the bibliography is at sources.html.

What is coming

Contributions are not open yet. The schema is still being tested against the first period, and migrating a hundred contributors' records is far harder than migrating your own — so the pipeline is built and tested before it is announced. When it opens, a form will build a bundle, validate it in your browser against the records already here, and open one issue; a person still reads the argument, the sources and the confidence before anything is merged. No sources, no merge.

How all of this is drawn — every symbol on the map, what the colours do and do not mean, how a card is read, what the lanes are, why the territories stop where they stop — is set out at length in how the atlas is drawn. The reasoning behind the project is in CONTEXT.md in the repository; the structure is in ARCHITECTURE.md; what is built so far is in STATUS.md.
