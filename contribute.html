Before you start

A person reads every contribution. This form does not write to the atlas. It builds a bundle of records, copies it to your clipboard and opens an issue with it. A maintainer reads the issue, and only when they apply the accepted label does anything become a pull request — which a person then reviews and merges. Nothing is automatic, and the review is the point: sources that really support the claim, an explanation that argues rather than asserts, the right edge type of the five.

No sources, no submit. Every event and every edge cites at least one source record, and the button below stays disabled until they all do. If the source is not yet in the bibliography, add it here in the same bundle: one bundle is one issue is one pull request, so an event, its edges and its new source travel together and are validated as a unit.

Write it yourself. The summary, the explanation and the dispute are the whole value of this project. Text generated by a language model is not accepted — not as a draft, not as a starting point. A model will produce a convincing causal story about any pair of events, including unrelated ones, which in a history site is total failure.

The three confidence levels

consensus
The link is accepted. It needs at least two cited sources by different authors — necessary, not sufficient: the reviewer still judges.
probable
Supported by the sources you cite, with no known dissent. If you are unsure whether historians disagree, say so in the explanation rather than choosing this by default.
disputed
Qualified historians disagree about the link itself: whether it exists, what type it is, or how much it carries. It stays in the atlas — removing it would be picking a side — is drawn differently, and must name who disagrees and cite them separately from the sources that support the link.

Licence

Records are published under CC BY-SA 4.0. Filing the issue grants that licence, and the issue template asks you to confirm it. The grant cannot be taken back: once other people have contributed on top of it, relicensing the data is impossible. Your name, as you give it below, and the GitHub account that opens the issue are recorded on every record you write — that is what attribution means here.

Contributions are not yet open to strangers: the schema is still being tested against the atlas's first period. The form works, and the pipeline behind it works, but issues filed now are for the people building it.
