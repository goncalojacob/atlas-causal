Before you start

A person reads every contribution. This form does not write to the atlas. It builds a bundle of records, copies it to your clipboard and opens an issue with it. A maintainer reads the issue. Only when they add the accepted label does the bundle become a pull request, and a person then reviews and merges it. Nothing happens automatically. The reviewer checks that the sources support the claim, that the explanation gives reasons for the link, and that the edge has the right one of the five types.

Every record needs a source. Every event, edge and actor cites at least one source record, and the button below stays disabled until they all do. If a source is not in the bibliography yet, add it here in the same bundle. One bundle makes one issue and one pull request. So an event, its edges and its new source travel together and pass the checks together.

Write it yourself. The summary, the explanation and the dispute are what this project is for. The atlas does not accept text from a language model, even as a draft or a starting point. A model will write a convincing causal story about any two events, even unrelated ones. On a history site that is a complete failure.

The three confidence levels

consensus
Historians accept the link. It needs at least two cited sources by different authors. Two sources are not enough on their own: the reviewer still judges.
probable
The sources you cite support the link, and you know of no historian who disagrees. If you are unsure whether historians disagree, say so in the explanation. Do not choose this level by default.
disputed
Historians disagree about the link itself: whether it exists, what type it is, or how much weight it carries. The link stays in the atlas, because removing it would pick a side. The atlas draws it differently. It must name who disagrees and cite them apart from the sources that support the link.

Licence

The atlas publishes records under CC BY-SA 4.0. Filing the issue grants that licence, and the issue template asks you to confirm it. You cannot take the grant back. Once other people have built on your records, nobody can change the licence of the data. Every record you write carries your name, as you give it below, and the GitHub account that opens the issue. That is the attribution.

Contributions are not open to the public yet. The maintainer is still testing the record format on the atlas's first period. The form and the process behind it work. For now, only the people building the atlas should file issues.
