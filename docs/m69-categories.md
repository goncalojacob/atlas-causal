# M69: the sixteen the category pass declined

M32b-2 gave 175 events a `category` and read, on purpose, **only a title an
import copied** (brief amendment A5). Of the 191 events the class table reached
then, sixteen had titles a person had written, and the pass listed them with
what the table would have said rather than writing it — because a pattern over
a composed title is a guess dressed as a rule, and the table files
`constitutional-revision-1959`, which is a law, as an `election`.

The owner's word of 20 September closed that question the other way round from
the one the pass left open: not "overrule amendment A5 and re-run the tool"
(which would still read the title), but **apply the table's answer to those
sixteen where the record's own body confirms it, and list the rest**.

So: the table's answer is the only answer any of these may be given. Where a
record's body called for a different category, the answer here is to refuse and
say so — deciding what such an event *was* is a person's to do and not this
run's, and writing a category the table does not give would be exactly the
composition amendment A5 refused.

Thirteen applied, three refused. Each applied record carries the flag
`m69-categorised` and nothing else changed: no summary, no date, no actor, no
source. The quotations below are the records' own summaries.

`germany-declares-war-1916` is the one of the thirteen worth arguing about: a
declaration of war is a diplomatic act, and `war` is "organised armed force
between polities". It is applied because the record is about a state of war
existing between two polities — its own summary says Portugal "was already
fighting German colonial forces in Angola and Mozambique" and that the
declaration "brought it formally into the European war" — and a reader
narrowing the atlas to `war` would be wrong not to find it.

## Refused — the table's answer is not what the body says

| Record | Table | Why not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `constitutional-revision-1959` | `election` | Its summary: "the constitution was revised so that the president would be chosen by an electoral college rather than by direct suffrage". That is the state changing what the rules are, which the vocabulary calls `law`. The table reached it on the word "elections" in a title about ending them. This is the owner's own example of the shape of mistake to refuse. |
| `covid-state-of-emergency-2020` | `disaster` | The event is not the epidemic. Its summary is a declaration and its renewals — "The president declared a state of emergency on 18 March 2020, the first under the 1976 constitution, and parliament renewed it twice"; "Movement, assembly and most economic activity were restricted by decree". `disaster` is "harm from something nobody chose"; this record is about what the state chose to do. |
| `portugal-backs-franco-1936` | `war` | The war in the title is somebody else's. The record is Portugal's policy towards it — "it allowed men and supplies to cross Portuguese territory, gave the rebels a diplomatic and broadcasting platform … and let some eight thousand volunteers … go and fight" — and it ends "It is in the atlas as the point where the regime's foreign policy is set for the next twenty years." The same shape as `war` inside "Warsaw", one word further out. |

Each of the three keeps no category at all. `category-unknown` is a warning and
not an error precisely because most of the corpus carries none, and a gap the
status file names is a correct atlas.

## What this run measured and did not act on

The class table reaches **307** events today and **94** of them carry no
category — not sixteen. Two things have happened since M32b-2 ran on 6
September:

- **The category pass has never been re-run since M40 and M42 imported the
  world.** Of the 94, about forty are active events whose titles an import
  copied — `korean-war`, `treaty-of-versailles`, `gulf-war` and the rest — and
  `tools/migrate/categories.mjs` would write a category for every one of them
  with no judgement required of anybody. That is a re-run of an existing tool
  and it is not in this brief, so this run did not do it.
- **Nine more hand-written titles have arrived** that the table reaches and
  that nobody has put to it: `1964-brazilian-coup-detat`,
  `1985-brazilian-presidential-election`,
  `cuban-war-of-independence-1895-1898`, `spanish-american-war-1898`,
  `strangford-treaty-1810`, `the-2018-brazilian-general-election`,
  `the-baptist-war-1831`, `the-rubber-battle-1942` and
  `treaty-of-tordesillas-1494`. They are the same kind of question as the
  sixteen and they are not the sixteen; the brief names the sixteen
  `STATUS.md` names, so these are reported and left.
