# M67 — the lines, and the umbrellas measured again with them in place

The owner, 20 September: *"you can do everything that is left including what
was in the backlog."* The first thing left is the one M65 named out loud. M65
made choosing an event a filter and put the resting picture at **the main
events only — 242 of 309** — and said plainly that a cut of a fifth *"will not
feel like much until far more of the 242 have somewhere to hang."*

**At the head this run started from: 309 active events, 242 of them main, and
48 of those 242 naming no actor and no place at all.** A record that names
nothing cannot be filed by any rule that reads a record, which is why M62 found
seven of them and had to leave them flat. This document is the measurement M51,
M58 and M62 made a habit of, and it was written and committed before a single
`parent` was.

## 1. The two jobs, and why they are in this order

M62's refusal of **World War I** is the whole argument for the order: sixteen
parentless events fell in its span and *"eight of them name no actor at all"*,
so the umbrella could not be built out of what the records said. The rule that
files a child reads `actors` and `place`. Where those are empty the rule is
blind, and no amount of measuring changes that.

So the lines come first, and the umbrellas are measured again afterwards.

## 2. Job one — the forty-eight

**Where the lines come from.** Every one of the 48 already cites `wikidata`,
and every line below is read off that item: `participant` (P710), `signatory`
(P1891), `location` (P276), `country` (P17), `successful candidate` (P991),
`candidate` (P726), `perpetrator` (P8031). The note beside each line says which
claim of which item it came from, so a reviewer can open the item and check the
line without reading this file. Where the item's `wikidata` citation carried no
locator, the item's own id was written into it, so the citation points at the
thing the line came from rather than at the database.

**No actor was created to make a line possible** — that is M42's job and the
brief says so. Where the item names a body this atlas has no record for, the
note says so and the line is left out. Where the atlas's record for a state
does not cover the year, another record for the same ground was used and the
note says which: `china` for the Qing dynasty in 1894 and 1911 (the atlas's
`qing-empire` runs 1783–1814, a Historical Basemaps snapshot), `persia` for
Iran before 1925 and `iran-persia` after it, `italy-sardinia` for the Kingdom
of Italy, as the corpus already does on **World War I** and **World War II**.
Nothing was written that would put an event outside the dates of an actor it
names — that warning, `actor-outside-when`, stood at zero on the head this run
started from and stands at zero now.

### 2.1 The two left bare, and why

- **`20-july-plot`.** Q105570 gives its participant as *"list of members of the
  20 July plot"*, which is an article and not a body; its target (P533) is
  Adolf Hitler, who has no record here; and its country (P17) is **Poland**,
  which is where the Wolf's Lair stands today and not the state the plot was
  against. There is no line to write that the item supports and the atlas can
  hold. Q105570 does put it inside **World War II** (P361), so the day somebody
  writes it an actor it will file itself.
- **`covid-19-pandemic`.** Q81068910 gives **119 countries** under P17 and one
  location, Wuhan, for which the atlas holds no place. A hundred and nineteen
  actor lines would say nothing, and picking one of them would be a claim the
  item does not make.

**So the events naming neither an actor nor a place fall from 48 to 2**, and
both of those are accounted for above.

## 3. Job two — the umbrellas, measured again

M62's rule stands unchanged: **inside the umbrella's span *and* inside its
subject**, where the subject is what the child's own `actors` or place says.
Necessary and not sufficient — the property is what a test can assert and the
filing is a judgement, written down case by case, as below. M62's two rules of
judgement are used again and are not restated:

1. **A period named for a form of government does not contain the act that
   created or destroyed it.** A war is different, because a war is made of its
   fighting.
2. **An event whose author is another state, with the umbrella as its object,
   is not part of the umbrella.**

### 3.1 World War I — re-measured, and it gains one

*Q361, 1914-07-28 – 1918-11-11. The umbrella already existed and kept the three
children M47 gave it.*

M62 refused it on the lines: **sixteen parentless events in its span, eight of
them naming no actor.** With the lines written, **eleven parentless events fall
in the span and the actor test returns five** — and four of those five were
already refused by M62 on grounds the lines do not touch:

| event | verdict |
| --- | --- |
| `arab-revolt` | **filed.** Q239060 puts it in the *Middle Eastern theater of World War I* (P361); it names `ottoman-empire`, a belligerent of the war; it is inside the span. |
| `assassination-of-archduke-franz-ferdinand` | refused, as in M62: 28 June 1914, a month before the war the record dates from 28 July. The act that brought a war about is not part of it. |
| `february-revolution` | refused, as in M62: its subject is the Russian Revolution, and it intersects only through `russian-empire`. |
| `german-revolution-of-1918-1919` | refused. It begins 4 November 1918, a week before the armistice, and Q170306 puts it inside the *revolutions of 1917–1923* and not inside the war. It outlives its would-be parent. |
| `treaty-of-london` | refused, and this is the arguable one. It is the secret treaty that brought Italy in, and no source this run read puts it *inside* the war. A flat event is honest. |
| `finnish-civil-war` | refused, though Q211855 does give World War I under P361 — because it gives the **Russian Civil War** there too, an event this atlas holds, and `parent` carries one. It goes there instead (§3.3). |

**So the lines changed exactly one thing about World War I**, and that is worth
saying plainly: the eight events M62 could not read were, once read, the
Portuguese elections of the First Republic and the revolutions and civil wars of
other countries. They had somewhere to go. It was mostly not the Great War.

### 3.3 The Russian chain — the civil war becomes an umbrella, and gains two

*`russian-civil-war`, 1917-11-07 – 1922-10-25, already an event here and until
now a childless one.* Thirteen parentless events fall in its span and the actor
test returns three.

| event | verdict |
| --- | --- |
| `finnish-civil-war` | **filed.** Q211855 gives the Russian Civil War under P361; it names `russian-sfsr`, the war's own actor; 27 January – 15 May 1918 is inside the span. |
| `polish-soviet-war` | **filed.** Q186284 gives the Russian Civil War under P361; it names `russian-sfsr`; 18 October 1920 – March 1921 is inside the span. |
| `october-revolution` | refused. Its record is dated 25–26 October 1917, which is the civil war's own start date of 7 November 1917 in the other calendar: the two records are all but the same days. It is the act that created the state the war was then fought over — judgement 1 — and no source read here puts it inside. |

Both children exist because of job one: neither named an actor this morning.

**The Soviet period, 1922-12-30 – 1991-12-26 (Q15180): refused.** 103
parentless events fall in the span and the actor test returns six —
`world-war-ii`, `spanish-civil-war`, `molotov-ribbentrop-pact`, `winter-war`,
`charter-of-the-united-nations` and `soviet-afghan-war`. Every one of them is a
war or a treaty the Soviet Union was a **party to**, and being a party to a
thing is not being part of a period. Nesting the Second World War inside the
Soviet Union would say something no historian says and this atlas cannot
defend. It is the Cold War refusal in another costume — and there is a second
reason: the Soviet Union is an **actor** here, a thing that acts, and it has
never needed an event for anything to be part of, the way Portuguese decrees
needed the Estado Novo. Refused.

### 3.4 The Brazilian chain — the Empire stands, the Old Republic does not

**`empire-of-brazil-1822-1889` is created.** *Q217230, inception 1822-09-07,
dissolved 1889-11-15, day precision, read 20 September 2026; "Empire of
Brazil", en revision 1374829984; "Império do Brasil", pt revision 72974929.*

**Subject:** the Brazilian state under the two emperors — its own laws, its
government's acts, its politics.

Seven parentless events fall in the span and the actor test returns five.

| event | verdict |
| --- | --- |
| `eusebio-de-queiros-law-1850` | **filed.** The Empire's own law, 4 September 1850, and the record names `empire-of-brazil` and nothing else. |
| `lei-aurea-1888` | **filed.** The Empire's own law, 13 May 1888, the same. |
| `independence-of-brazil-1822` | refused: the act that created it (judgement 1). |
| `proclamation-of-the-brazilian-republic-1889` | refused: the act that destroyed it (judgement 1). |
| `aberdeen-act-1845` | refused: an act of the United Kingdom's parliament with the Empire as its object (judgement 2), and its place is London. |

The other two inside the window, `the-baptist-war-1831` and
`slavery-abolition-act-1833`, are British Caribbean and not Brazil.

**Two children is thin**, and it is the same thinness M62 accepted for the
Brazilian military dictatorship and for the Ditadura Nacional, for the same
reason: it is a real period with a sourced span that the Brazilian records will
attach to, and the only way to make it look fuller would be to file inside it
the two acts that begin and end it. The two long processes of those years —
`the-brazilian-coffee-cycle` (1830–1930) and `the-amazon-rubber-boom`
(1879–1912) — straddle the Empire's end and would each be the
`child-outside-parent` warning this milestone must not produce.

**The First Brazilian Republic, 1889-11-15 – 1930-10-24 (Q2414171): refused.**
Fifty-two parentless events fall in the span and the actor test returns two, of
which one, `proclamation-of-the-brazilian-republic-1889`, is its own founding
act. **One child under one new umbrella reorganises nothing** — it costs a main
event to save a main event — and the events of the Brazilian Old Republic this
atlas holds are the two long cycles above, which straddle its boundaries at
both ends. The umbrella can be written the day the records are there for it.

### 3.6 The Balkan Wars — measured, and refused on a date

*Q165725, P580 1912-10-08, P582 **1913-07-18**.* Three parentless events fall in
that span and the actor test returns two: `first-balkan-war` and
`second-balkan-war`, both of which Q177918 and Q184183 put inside the Balkan
Wars under P361, and both of which now name the same five belligerents. On
every other count this is the strongest candidate this run found.

It is refused because **the source contradicts itself about the end**: Q184183
ends the Second Balkan War on **1913-08-10**, three weeks after Q165725 ends the
pair. Writing the umbrella on Wikidata's own span would put a child's last day
outside its parent's; writing it on the child's would be a date this run made
up. The year bound the validator reads is 1913 either way, so the warning would
not fire — which is exactly why it is worth refusing out loud rather than
quietly. The same discipline refused the PREC in M62.

### 3.7 The Cold War and the PREC

Untouched, as the brief instructs. *"Part of the Cold War" is the
historiographical argument*, and an argument belongs in an edge with an
explanation and a confidence. The PREC's span two sources disagree about, and
`parent` cannot carry a hedge.

about why anything happened — only who did it and where.
