# Writing rules

The owner, 13 October 2026: "All text you write should be plain and simple,
easy to understand without jargon, redundancy and unnecessary words. You
should avoid any AI slop." The rules below distil the owner's rulebook on AI
slop. They apply to every sentence a person or a routine writes in this
repository: documents, briefs, notes, commit messages, accounts, the site's
own prose, and the explanation on an edge. They do not apply to text quoted
from a source, which stays as the source wrote it.

## Words

1. Use the plain word. Predict, not anticipate. Growth, not expansion.
   Improve, not optimize. Consultants, not FTEs. If a more ordinary word
   carries the same meaning, use it. A term being standard in a field is no
   defence.
2. Name a specific thing. A reader outside the field must be able to say
   what a noun phrase refers to. "Service quality", "the network", "capacity",
   "momentum" and "alignment" name nothing. "Larger tanks and more frequent
   refuelling" names something.
3. Cut adjectives that carry no fact: key, significant, robust, strategic,
   extensive, best-in-class, fully, ideally. Delete the word and check that
   no fact was lost.
4. No slogans, no promotion, no triads of abstractions ("faster, smarter and
   more intuitive"). Describe the thing as it is.
5. No jargon from the project's own history where a plain phrase exists.
   Write "the rule that an event needs an edge", not "the bar". Write the
   milestone's subject, not only its number.

## Sentences

6. Short sentences, one idea each. If a sentence can be shorter without
   losing a fact, shorten it. Figures and necessary technical detail are not
   padding.
7. Active voice with the actor named. "The writer imports the item", not
   "the item is imported".
8. No "X, not Y" and no "not X but Y" contrasts. Say what it is.
9. Say a thing once. Two words in one sentence carrying the same idea are
   one too many ("cumulative total", "historical past").
10. Cut qualifiers the context already gives. Inside a section about Africa,
    "in Africa" is noise.
11. One kind of content per bullet. Advice and analysis are two bullets.
12. One modal hedge is fine. Stacked hedging ("there may be times when one
    might consider") is noise.

## Punctuation

13. No em dashes, no semicolons, no vertical bars, no arrows, no in-line
    bullets. Use a full stop or a comma.
14. One colon may introduce a list or a lead-in. Two colons in one element,
    each followed by a fragment, are a slogan.
15. Parentheses, forward slashes, en dashes in ranges and ampersands
    between related things are fine.

## Structure

16. A heading names its section. It does not sell it and it does not repeat
    the title in other words.
17. A line that does not belong to its section goes to its own section or
    goes.
18. Before finishing, run every rule that fired on one line across every
    other line.

## What stays as it is

19. Quotes from sources: a Wikipedia sentence in a summary or an edge's
    explanation, a passage of a brief that quotes the owner.
20. Identifiers: record ids, field names, rule numbers, file paths, commands.
21. Technical density that carries information: a sentence long because it
    is full of concrete figures is not padding.
