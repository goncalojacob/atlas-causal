# M40b retractions — imported world events that could not be wired

Every event listed here was imported by M40a and is now `retracted`. The reason
is on the record too, in `review.note`, so the review dashboard shows it
without this file. None of them was `merged`: not one of the 91 world events
duplicated a record the atlas already held under another id, which is what the
M40a reconciliation rule was for.

The bar is the owner's one-edge rule of 4 September 2026, as `docs/m22-brief.md`
records it: **one honest edge, in either direction, is enough to keep an event;
only an event with no honest edge at all is retracted; and no edge is ever
written in order to keep an event.** An edge is honest here when it can be
argued from what this atlas holds — the cached leads of the two records it
joins, and the books in the bibliography. Twenty-eight of ninety-one are
retracted, a rate of thirty-one per cent.

Retraction is not a judgement that an event is unimportant. Several of these
are among the largest events of the century; the Rwandan genocide and
Srebrenica are here. It is a judgement that *this atlas, with the records and
the books it currently holds*, cannot say what followed from them without
making something up.

Three causes account for most of the list, and they are worth stating because
they are findings about the import rather than about history:

1. **The neighbours are missing.** The import's rule kept the 120 candidates
   with the most sitelinks that the atlas did not already hold, with a floor of
   six per decade. That rule selects events that are famous, and famous events
   are connected to other famous events — most of which were not kept, or were
   refused. So the atlas holds the Velvet Revolution and not the fall of the
   Wall; the second Chechen war and not the first; the second
   Nagorno-Karabakh war and not the first; the Libyan and Syrian civil wars
   and not the Arab Spring; the war in Afghanistan and not 11 September.
   **Eight of the twenty-eight retractions name an event that was among the
   twenty-nine the import refused for want of a lane** (`STATUS.md`, deviation
   192). Importing those twenty-nine would turn much of this list back into
   records with edges.
2. **Multilateral conventions have no consequences this atlas can see.**
   Ramsar, CEDAW, the rights of the child, the law of the sea, the
   non-proliferation treaty: five of the retractions. What they changed is
   administrative, and Portugal's part in them is ratification and compliance,
   which this dataset has no records of. The covenant of 1966 was kept
   because it is the direct binding successor of a Charter this atlas does
   hold, and the note on CEDAW says so.
3. **Whole regions are absent.** No Latin America outside Brazil, no Balkans
   after 1923, no Great Lakes, no Sudan, no Caucasus, no Turkey between
   Lausanne and 2016, no American domestic politics, and nothing in China
   between 1949 and 1999.

Where a retraction is worth revisiting, the entry says what the atlas would
need first.

---
## 1890s
- `anglo-zanzibar-war` — Retracted in M40b: the shortest war on record, forty minutes on 27 August 1896 over who would succeed as Sultan of Zanzibar. The atlas holds no event in East Africa before 1953 and nothing about the British protectorate, so there is nothing the war can be argued to have changed here and nothing here that can be argued to have caused it. It would need a record of the 1890 protectorate agreement, or of the abolition of slavery in Zanzibar in 1897, before it had an honest edge.

## 1900s
- `ilinden-preobrazhenie-uprising` — Retracted in M40b: the rising of the Internal Macedonian-Adrianople Revolutionary Organization against Ottoman rule, August to October 1903. The line historians draw from it to the Committee of Union and Progress is real, but nothing this atlas holds argues it: the cached leads say only what the rising was. It would need a work on the late Ottoman Balkans in the bibliography before the edge to 1908 could be written rather than assumed.

## 1910s
- `mexican-revolution` — Retracted in M40b: ten years of civil war from November 1910, the destruction of the Federal Army and the constitution of 1917. The atlas holds no record in Latin America outside Brazil and no record of United States intervention anywhere, so there is nothing here the revolution can be argued to have changed and nothing here that can be argued to have caused it. The coincidence with the Portuguese republic of October 1910 is a coincidence, and an edge saying otherwise would be invented.
- `russian-revolution` — Retracted in M40b: the umbrella item for the Russian Revolution of 1917. The atlas already holds the thing itself in three records with dates, actors and edges of their own — the February revolution, the October revolution and the civil war — and a fourth node covering all three would double every path through them in the graph and the timeline without adding an argument. Nothing here is lost: the period is walkable from February to October to the civil war.

## 1920s
- `lateran-treaty` — Retracted in M40b: the settlement of the Roman question between Mussolini's Italy and the Holy See in February 1929. The link that would matter is the Portuguese one — the Concordat and Missionary Accord of 1940, negotiated in the same spirit — and the atlas holds no record of either, nor any other Italian or papal event before 2023. Wiring it would mean inventing a link across seventy years. It needs the Concordat of 1940 as a record first.

## 1930s
- `holodomor` — Retracted in M40b: the famine of 1932–33 in Soviet Ukraine, part of the wider Soviet famine of 1930–33 and now recognised by many states as a genocide. Its causes are collectivisation, the grain procurement quotas and the closing of the republic's borders, and the atlas holds no record of any of them; its consequences here would be a question of memory and recognition, decades later, which this atlas has no records for either. Wiring it to the civil war or to October would be an invention.
- `anti-comintern-pact` — Retracted in M40b: the German-Japanese agreement of November 1936 against the Communist International, joined by Italy in 1937 and by Spain and Hungary in 1939. The atlas holds no record of the formation of the Axis — no Pact of Steel, no Tripartite Pact — and Portugal never adhered. Its clearest documented consequence, the fall of the Hiranuma cabinet in Tokyo when Berlin signed with Moscow in August 1939, is also not a record here, and an edge to it would in any case run backwards.

## 1950s
- `hungarian-revolution-of-1956` — Retracted in M40b: the rising of October 1956 against the Hungarian government and the Soviet presence, crushed by the Red Army in a fortnight. The atlas holds no record of the eastern bloc's internal history — no Berlin 1953, no Prague 1968 — for it to connect to, and its clearest reach into Portugal, the crisis it caused in the western communist parties and so in the PCP, would need that crisis as a record here. Wiring it forward to 1989 across thirty-three years would be an invention.

## 1960s
- `assassination-of-john-f-kennedy` — Retracted in M40b: Kennedy shot in Dallas on 22 November 1963. The Portuguese link would be American policy on the African territories — the vote against Portugal in the Security Council in 1961, and the renewal of the Azores base agreement used against it — and the atlas holds no record of either, so there is nothing here the death can be argued to have changed. It would need the Azores lease of 1962 as a record before the change of American tone after 1963 could be argued rather than asserted.
- `cultural-revolution` — Retracted in M40b: the decade of upheaval Mao launched in May 1966. The atlas holds nothing in China between 1949 and 1999, and the link that would matter here is precise: the spill-over into Macau in December 1966, after which Portuguese authority in the territory was nominal and the question of returning it was open. No source this atlas holds carries that incident, so the edge would rest on nothing. It needs the 1-2-3 incident of 1966–67 as a record first.
- `treaty-on-the-non-proliferation-of-nuclear-weapons` — Retracted in M40b: the non-proliferation treaty opened for signature on 1 July 1968. Its causes are the Chinese test of 1964 and the missile crisis of 1962, and its consequences are the safeguards system and the tests that broke it; the atlas holds no record of the nuclear age at all. Portugal ratified in 1977, having never had a programme, and there is no Portuguese record the treaty can be argued to have changed.

## 1970s
- `ramsar-convention` — Retracted in M40b: the 1971 convention on wetlands of international importance. The atlas holds no environmental record before the fires of 2017 and nothing at all on conservation, so there is no record here the convention can be argued to have changed and none that produced it. Portugal acceded in 1980 and has designated sites under it since, none of which is an event this atlas holds. It would need a Portuguese conservation record first.
- `convention-on-the-elimination-of-all-forms-of-discrimination-against-women` — Retracted in M40b: CEDAW, adopted by the General Assembly in December 1979. Its lineage runs through the Commission on the Status of Women and the declaration of 1967, neither of which the atlas holds — unlike the covenant of 1966, which is the direct binding successor of the Charter's own promise and is wired to it on that ground. Its Portuguese reach would be the family law reform of 1977 and the equality legislation that followed, and the atlas holds no record of either.

## 1980s
- `falklands-war` — Retracted in M40b: ten weeks in the South Atlantic in 1982 between Argentina and the United Kingdom. The atlas holds no record anywhere in South America outside Brazil, and none of the Argentine dictatorship, so the war's largest consequence — the collapse of the junta and the return to civilian rule in 1983 — is not a record here. There is no Portuguese link at all. It would need the Argentine transition as a record before it had an honest edge.
- `united-nations-convention-on-the-law-of-the-sea` — Retracted in M40b: the law of the sea convention signed at Montego Bay in December 1982. Portugal is one of the states it matters most to — the exclusive economic zone, and the extended continental shelf claim filed in 2009 — and the atlas holds no record of any of that, so the convention has nothing here to be wired to. Expo 98 and its ocean theme are not a consequence of it. It would need the continental shelf claim, or the zone itself, as a record.
- `convention-on-the-rights-of-the-child` — Retracted in M40b: the convention on the rights of the child, adopted in November 1989 and the most widely ratified treaty there is. As with CEDAW, its lineage — the declaration of 1959 and the drafting group of the 1980s — is not in this atlas, and its Portuguese reach would be legislation on children, the family and schooling that no record here holds. The covenant of 1966 was kept because it is the direct binding successor of the Charter this atlas does hold.
- `velvet-revolution` — Retracted in M40b: the Czechoslovak revolution of November 1989. The atlas's whole 1989 is this one national revolution — the fall of the Wall, German unification and the dissolution of the Soviet Union are not records here, and the Cold War itself was refused by the import for want of a lane. The argument that would matter, 1989 as the end of the Cold War, has no node to run from, and running it from Prague alone would credit one revolution with the year.

## 1990s
- `1994-genocide-against-tutsi` — Retracted in M40b: the killing of at least half a million Tutsi in Rwanda between April and July 1994. The atlas holds no record of the Rwandan civil war, the Arusha accords, the United Nations mission that was not reinforced, or the wars in Congo that followed, and there is no Portuguese link. Its reach into the rest of this dataset would be through the doctrine of protection that Rwanda and Srebrenica together produced, and that doctrine has no record here either.
- `srebrenica-massacre` — Retracted in M40b: the killing of more than eight thousand Bosniak men and boys in July 1995, the first legally recognised genocide in Europe since 1945. The atlas holds nothing else in the Balkans after 1923 — no Bosnian war, no Dayton, no tribunal at The Hague, and the Yugoslav wars as a series were among the candidates the import refused for want of a lane. There is no Portuguese link. It would need the Bosnian war as a record before it had an honest edge.
- `second-chechen-war` — Retracted in M40b: the Russian campaign in Chechnya from August 1999. The first Chechen war was among the twenty-nine candidates the import refused for want of a lane, the dissolution of the Soviet Union is not a record here, and neither is Putin's accession — which is where the argument that this war made a career would have to run. There is no Portuguese link. It would need the first war, or the presidency of 2000, as a record.

## 2000s
- `war-in-afghanistan-2001-2021` — Retracted in M40b: twenty years of war from October 2001 to the Taliban's return in August 2021. Its cause is the attacks of 11 September 2001, which is not a record here — the War on Terrorism as a series was among the twenty-nine candidates the import refused for want of a lane — and its consequences in this dataset are none. Portugal sent forces under ISAF for most of the war and the atlas holds no record of that either. It would need 11 September first.

## 2010s
- `libyan-civil-war` — Retracted in M40b: the rising against Gaddafi from February 2011 and the NATO-supported war that ended with his death in October. Its cause, the Arab Spring, was among the twenty-nine candidates the import refused for want of a lane; its consequences — the second Libyan war, the collapse of the state, the central Mediterranean route — are not records here, and neither is the Security Council's later refusal over Syria, which is the argument that would have run out of it.
- `syrian-civil-war` — Retracted in M40b: fourteen years from the protests of March 2011 to the fall of Assad in December 2024. As with Libya, its cause is the Arab Spring, which the import refused for want of a lane. Everything it can be argued to have changed in this dataset is absent: the Islamic State, the refugee movement of 2015 and the European politics that followed it, and the Russian intervention of 2015. It would need the Arab Spring, or the refugee crisis, as a record.
- `paris-agreement` — Retracted in M40b: the climate agreement of December 2015. The atlas holds no climate or energy record at all — the Kyoto Protocol was among the candidates the import refused for want of a lane, and the Portuguese fires of 2017 are wired to the reform they produced and not to any climate policy. There is nothing here the agreement can be argued to have changed and nothing here that produced it.
- `2016-turkish-coup-d-etat-attempt` — Retracted in M40b: the failed coup of 15 July 2016 and the purges that followed it. The atlas holds nothing in Turkey between the treaty of Lausanne in 1923 and this night, so there is no record here the attempt can be argued to have changed and none that produced it, and no Portuguese link at all. It would need the Turkish republic's own history — the coups of 1960, 1971 and 1980 at least — before it had an honest edge.

## 2020s
- `second-nagorno-karabakh-war` — Retracted in M40b: forty-four days in 2020 in which Azerbaijan retook most of Nagorno-Karabakh. The first war over the same ground was among the twenty-nine candidates the import refused for want of a lane, and the atlas holds nothing else in the Caucasus and no record of the dissolution of the Soviet Union that produced the dispute. There is no Portuguese link. It would need the first war as a record before it had an honest edge.
- `january-6-united-states-capitol-attack` — Retracted in M40b: the attack on the United States Capitol on 6 January 2021. The atlas holds no record of American domestic politics at all — no presidential election, no administration — so there is nothing here the attack can be argued to have changed and nothing that produced it. The United States appears in this dataset only as a belligerent, a signatory and a negotiator in other people's events.
- `sudanese-civil-war` — Retracted in M40b: the war between the army and the Rapid Support Forces since April 2023. The atlas holds nothing in Sudan — not the Darfur war out of which the RSF came, not the revolution of 2019, not the secession of South Sudan — and there is no Portuguese link. It would need the Sudanese revolution, or Darfur, as a record before an honest edge could be written from either end.
