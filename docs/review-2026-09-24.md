## 1. Panning the graph reveals blank space: nothing is drawn again after a drag

**Severity: bug (visible in the demo the moment a reader zooms and drags).**
`src/graph-view/graph-view.js` 565–595 (the pointer handlers), 681–687 (a
click after a drag returns), 963–964 (the cull), 950–955 (the comment that
says a pan redraws); compare `src/map/map.js` 456–461 and 350–356.

What happens: since I6 the graph puts in the DOM only the stacks inside the
rectangle on screen plus `DRAW_MARGIN / k` (963–964). The comment above it
says *"The rectangle is already in this view's render key, so a pan redraws
and the cull can never leave a stale picture behind"* — true of the map, whose
`pointerup` schedules a `bbox` publish (map.js 460 → 355 `state.set`) that
reaches `render` through the store. The graph has no bbox: `pointermove`
applies the transform and nothing else (587–589), `pointerup` releases the
capture and clears `drag` (591–595), and the `click` that follows returns at
684–687 because `dragged` is set. `render` is called from the wheel (617), a
double-click (625), a stack zoom (551), the observer (1393) and the store —
never from a drag. So: wheel in (or open a stack, which zooms to it), drag
towards the part of the picture you wanted, and the ground there is empty
until the next notch, click or state change. At rest nothing is culled
(the frame fits the whole picture), which is why no screenshot shows it; every
zoomed pan does. No browser test pans the graph (`tests/graph-browser.test.mjs`
zooms ten notches at 445–460 and never drags).

Fix: in `pointerup`, when `drag.moved`, call `render(state.get())` — the key
carries the rectangle (850–851), so it draws exactly once and only if the
box moved; better, during `pointermove` book one `render` per animation frame
so the marks appear as the drag goes, the way the map's marks do at the bbox
settle. Add to `graph-browser` a test that zooms, drags by half the pane and
asserts every stack inside the new rectangle is in the DOM (the `DRAWING`
helper at 400–420 already reads the rectangle and the nodes). Changes nothing
the atlas says.

## 2. A resize throws away the camera the reader set, and that is the ring-ratio flake

**Severity: bug (small for a reader; the cause of a red CI lane).**
`src/graph-view/graph-view.js` 1338–1339 (`key` and the early return),
1349–1351, 1386–1394 (the observer); `tests/graph-browser.test.mjs` 365–379;
`index.html` 54 with `src/style.css` 308 (the chips live in the masthead,
which wraps).

What happens: `frameCamera` keys on the question *and the rectangle*
(1338) and returns early only when the key is unchanged (1339). Deviation
1176's `cameraMoved` guards the case "same key, new layout"; it does not guard
"same question, new rectangle". Any resize — the panel opening on the first
card, the masthead gaining a row when a lens chip's title lands, the window
being resized, the phone's sheet — fires the observer (1388), which nulls the
measurement and renders; `seen` differs, the key differs, and the frame
overwrites a camera the reader wheeled or dragged into place (1377), against
the promise the comment above it makes ("never to one the reader wheeled or
dragged into place").

That is what `graph-browser` 334 measured on PR #24: the assertion at 379
compares the ring's stroke after a wheel of `deltaY = -600` (factor
e^0.9 = 2.46) with the stroke at rest. The failing values were 0.77 against
0.58, i.e. k = 1.30 after the "wheel" and 1.72 at rest. A wheel of 2.46 on
any camera at or above `MIN_ZOOM = 1` leaves k ≥ 2.46; 1.30 cannot be a
post-wheel camera, it is a *fit* — the frame re-ran after the wheel. The
likeliest resize: `open(... selected=fixture-event-f ...)` opens a lens, its
chip is drawn in `#lens-chips` inside the flex-wrapping masthead when the
title lands, the masthead grows a row, the graph's pane loses it. The test
waits for `NODE('fixture-event-t')` (a draw) and not for the frame to settle.

Two residuals of B9 above 600 events (latent today, live at ~846 main):
`arrange` returns `laid !== null` while the Worker runs (468), so the *old*
picture is drawn under the new question with no "Arranging the graph…" note
(459 shows it only when there is no `laid` at all); and a reader who wheels
during that wait sets `cameraMoved`, so when the new layout — new
coordinates — lands, 1339 returns and it is drawn at a camera set on the old
one, possibly showing nothing.

Fix: (a) in `frameCamera`, when only the rectangle part of the key changed and
`cameraMoved` is true, keep the camera and translate it so the graph point at
the old rectangle's centre stays at the new one's; (b) record which layout the
camera was moved on (`movedOn = laid` in the wheel/drag handlers) and let the
re-fit through when `laid !== movedOn`; (c) show `waiting` whenever a job is
pending (`runner.run` was called and has not answered), not only when `laid`
is null. In the test: before the wheel, wait until the lens chip is named
(`named('#lens-chips .lens-name')`) and the viewport transform reads the same
on two frames; read k then; assert `parted.stroke < 1 / k_before` rather than
against the other page's rest. Changes nothing the atlas says.

## 3. The graph's degree control does nothing whenever anything is selected

**Severity: weakness (a control that does nothing, with no sign that it does
nothing).** `src/graph-filters.js` 53–71 (never disabled); `src/graph-view/
arrangement.js` 199 (`view ? kept : kept.filter(organises)`); `src/lens.js`
481–483 (any selected active event is an implicit lens: `eventsOfFocus` of
kind `event` always holds the event itself, so its size is ≥ 1);
`src/window-control.js` 405–410 (the count follows the arrangement, so it
reads the same).

What happens: the floor is by rule off inside a lens (M48 §3). Since H7/M65
every selection *is* a lens, so the select in the masthead — "Show events with
at least 2 connections" — changes nothing on the picture or the count from the
moment a reader clicks any node until they click the ground. It changes the
URL (`?degree=2`) and comes back to life when the selection is put down, which
reads as a control that works sometimes. M85 kept it on the measurement that
it does something at rest (232/154/82), which is right; at rest is the only
time it does.

Fix: disable the `<select>` while `lensView(atlas, s)` is non-null, with a
title that says why ("Off while an event is open: what is drawn is that event
and its parts"), and re-enable at rest; or hide the group under a lens the way
`layersGroup` is hidden off the map (main.js 327). Changes nothing the atlas
says.

## 4. First paint at the whole span is up to twelve full redraws of every picture

**Severity: weakness (structural; linear in the corpus).** `src/main.js`
380–393 (`shardLanded`, `askFor`), 431–439 (`holdWindow` asks for every shard
the window covers, which since M85 is all of them), 445 (asks for all of them
again); compare `src/map/map.js` 838–851 (`baseArrived` coalesces with
`defer`).

What happens: the atlas opens on 1492–2026, so `attributeShardsIn` returns all
ten century shards plus `null` and `place` (12 files, 1.0 MB today) and each
lands in its own microtask. Every landing runs `remeasure()` — the map (a
cluster pass, the label round, the corner), the timeline (a full repack of the
rows, see 5), the graph (a label round), the band, the masthead control — plus
`panel.refresh`, the chips, the intro and the composer, and `applyShard` runs
`atlas.reindexRecords()` (eight index fills over every record) before each.
Twelve of everything inside the first second, on every visit. Today the
pieces are cheap (the resting layout is 25 ms, the graph's label round 14 ms,
`stackLayout` 3 ms, measured); at 3,000 events they are three to four times
that, and the timeline's repack is the one that shows. The keyboard flake
(finding 6) and the band-drag flake M85 §9 fixed are both "a later shard
repacked what the test had read"; fewer, later, batched repaints would shrink
that window as well.

Fix: coalesce `shardLanded` per animation frame exactly as `baseArrived` does
(`pending` flag, `requestAnimationFrame` then `setTimeout`), so the twelve
landings of a first paint become one to three redraws; call
`atlas.reindexRecords()` once per batch rather than per file (the cap's
eviction already does it separately). Line 445 is redundant at the whole
span — harmless, since `loadAttributes` deduplicates in flight (data.js
1243–1262). Changes nothing the atlas says.

## 5. At the whole span the timeline is a 2,500-pixel list, and it grows one row per title

**Severity: weakness (structural; at 3,000 events it is a 9,000-pixel page).**
`src/timeline.js` 660–681 (`rowLanes` with no row cap, `laneHeightFor` with
`minimum: ROW_HEIGHT`, `height = max(rows × laneHeight, paneHeight)`), the
observer's own comment at 849–852 ("the drawing is regularly taller than its
pane"); `src/lanes.js` 168–170 (`maxRows = Infinity` and the timeline passes
none), 234–244 (the row-limited branch that already exists).

Computed with `rowLanes` over today's resting set, the timeline's own scale
and `labelRoom`: **242 main events → 112 rows at 1280 px (2,542 px tall), 136
rows at 960 px (3,070 px)**. The pane is about 700 px, so a reader at rest sees
the first thirty rows and a scrollbar; `docs/screens/m84-timeline-ring.png`
shows the same shape under a lens (one bar per row, thirty rows for 53
events). Linear in the number of main events: at ~846 it is ~390–475 rows,
8,700–10,500 px. Every band drag repacks all of it (`draw` is called on each
`pointermove` through the store).

Fix: cap the rows at what the pane holds (`maxRows` from `paneHeight /
ROW_HEIGHT`, which `packRows` already honours by reusing the earliest-ending
row) and, past the cap, stop reserving label room — a bar that will not get a
label should not take a row for it; label the rows that fit, as the graph
