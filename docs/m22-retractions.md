# M22 retractions — imported events from 1975 on that could not be wired

Every event listed here was imported by M20 and is now `retracted` (or
`merged`, where the atlas already held the same event under another id). The
reason is on the record too, in `review.note`, so the review dashboard shows
it without this file.

The bar M22 works to is **not** the one in its own brief. The brief asks for
two argued edges per event, one of them outgoing; on 4 September 2026 the
owner replaced that with a single rule for M21 and M22 alike: **one honest
edge, in either direction, is enough to keep an event; only an event with no
honest edge at all is retracted; and no edge is ever written in order to keep
an event.** The bar that matters is therefore the second half of it. An event
survives here if there is one link this atlas can argue from the books it
holds — and it is retracted when the only links available would be guesses.

Where a retraction is worth revisiting later, the entry says what the atlas
would need first.

## 1970s — 14 of 26 retracted, 3 merged, 9 wired

Two groups account for all fourteen.

**The armed far left, eleven records.** The import brought a dense series of
bank robberies and killings by the PRP-BR and its Brigadas Revolucionárias,
1975 to 1979, each with a date, a branch and a sum in escudos. They are real
and they are documented, and the atlas cannot use one of them, because the
atlas holds no record of the organisations themselves — not the PRP, not the
Brigadas Revolucionárias, not the FP-25 that came after them, not the trials.
There is nothing for an action of theirs to attach to in either direction. An
edge to 25 April or to 25 November would not be an argument about these
robberies; it would be an argument about the armed left in general, made by
pointing at a bank in Seia. Retracting them is a statement about a gap in the
atlas rather than about the events.

What the atlas would need first: an actor for the PRP-BR, an event for the
turn to armed action after 25 November 1975, and an event for the trials — at
which point most of these eleven become evidence for edges rather than
orphans.

  21 October 1975, six of the Brigadas Revolucionárias, 4,237,000$00.

**Three items with no label.** `q55499125` (31 August 1975), `q55499135`
(1 September 1975) and `q5941661` (27 August 1977) came in with a date, a
region of Asia and nothing else: no label in English or Portuguese, no
description, no article in any language. Nothing can be said about them,
which means nothing can be argued from them either. Each keeps its Wikidata
id, so a label appearing upstream is enough to revive the record.

The weakest of the nine is `hat-nipah-and-same-massacres`, and it carries a
`facts-thin` flag saying so: the import brought no lead for it and the
bibliography holds no work on the occupation of East Timor, so the record
asserts the date, the two places and the fact of the killings and explicitly
refuses to assert who carried them out. Its one edge argues the circumstance —
the war opened by the invasion of December 1975 — and not the hand. A reviewer
who cannot establish the rest should retract it.
