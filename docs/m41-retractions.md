# M41b retractions — imported records that could not be joined

Every record listed here was imported by M41a and is now `retracted`. The reason
is on the record too, in `review.note`, so the review dashboard shows it without
this file.

**This list is not shaped like `docs/m40-retractions.md`, because the round it
came out of was not shaped like M40a's.** M40a imported ninety-one *events* and
M40b asked of each one whether an honest edge could be drawn. The second
Portuguese round returned forty-one **actors** and one event: every actor an
`institution`, and ninety-five of the hundred and fifty candidates a company or
an institution founding. Deviation 207 in `STATUS.md` says why. An actor takes
no edge — an edge runs between events — so the one-edge rule had to be read
across into the two things an actor record can honestly be attached by:

- an **actor line on an event the atlas already holds**, where that event's own
  prose already names the actor, or where the actor's own cached lead says
  plainly that it took part in that event; and
- a **relation** to an actor the atlas already holds, of the six types, cited
  like any other relation.

Nothing else counts, and neither was written in order to keep a record. A
`part-of` from every company to the Portuguese state would have cleared all
twenty-four of these warnings and said nothing; it is exactly the move the
one-edge rule exists to forbid.

Two of the forty-two were not retracted but **merged**, because the import's
reconciliation had missed that the atlas already held them: `national-union` is
the `uniao-nacional` of M7, and `democratic-party` is `partido-democratico`.
Both name their survivor and keep the Wikidata id the import brought, as M22's
merged elections do.

Sixteen were kept: fifteen actors and the one event. **Twenty-four of
forty-two are retracted, a rate of fifty-seven per cent** — nearly twice
M40b's thirty-one, and the number is a fact about the queries rather than
about the records.

Three causes account for the whole list.

1. **The atlas has no economic or industrial history of Portugal after 1975.**
   Fourteen of the twenty-four are companies. It holds the nationalisations of
   March 1975 and it holds nothing of what undid them: no privatisation, no
   banking law, no telecommunications liberalisation, no motorway programme, no
   stock exchange. So EDP, made in 1976 out of the electricity companies the
   1975 nationalisations took, has a real link to a record here and no event to
   attach it to; and Galp, BCP, BPI, Brisa and the rest are further from the
   atlas than EDP is, not nearer.
2. **A party is joined to the atlas by an election, and the atlas holds the
   elections it holds.** Nine of the twenty-four are parties or movements. The
   ones that were kept were kept because a record already named them — the
   Republican Liberals in 1921, the CDE in 1969 and 1973, the PPM in 1979, the
   Greens in 2015, Iniciativa Liberal in 2019 — or because a merger joined them
   to one that was. LIVRE, Volt, Ergue-te and the PLS are named by no record
   here and by no lead of their own; the Nationalist Party of 1903 has one
   Portuguese sentence cached for it and no English article at all.
3. **Two are outside what this atlas covers.** Euronext is not a Portuguese
   body, and everything the New Macau Association's leads describe happened
   after 20 December 1999, which is where the Portuguese record of Macau ends.

Where a retraction is worth revisiting, the entry says what the atlas would
need first. The one most worth undoing is the National Syndicalists.

## Companies and infrastructure

- `energias-de-portugal` — Retracted in M41b: EDP, the electricity utility founded in 1976 out of the merger of fourteen nationalised companies. The link to the atlas is real and it has no record to hang on: the nationalisations this atlas holds are those of March 1975, a year earlier, and EDP was not an actor in them but a body made afterwards out of what they took. It would need an event for the creation of EDP in 1976, or for the nationalisation of electricity that followed the banks over that summer.
- `galp-energia` — Retracted in M41b: Galp, formed in 1999 by the merger of Petrogal, Gás de Portugal and Transgás, and the largest oil and gas group in the country. The same gap as EDP's and one step further away: Petrogal was itself made out of the nationalisations, and the atlas holds neither its creation nor Galp's. Nothing here can be argued to have caused or been caused by a merger of holding companies in 1999.
- `banco-comercial-portugues` — Retracted in M41b: BCP, founded in 1985 in the liberalisation that followed nearly a decade of state control of Portuguese banking, and now the largest private bank. The atlas holds the nationalisation of the banks in 1975 and the resolution of Banco Espírito Santo in 2014, and BCP was an actor in neither. It would need a record of the banking law of the mid-1980s, or of the privatisations of the 1990s, before it had anything to be joined to.
- `portuguese-investment-bank` — Retracted in M41b: Banco BPI, founded in 1981 as Banco Português de Investimento and now owned by CaixaBank. The atlas holds no event of the Portuguese banking system between the nationalisations of 1975 and the resolution of BES in 2014 that BPI took any part in, and the leads say nothing that could be argued from. Same gap as BCP's.
- `euronext` — Retracted in M41b: the pan-European exchange that operates the Lisbon bourse among seven others, spun off from ICE in 2014. The atlas holds nothing about the Lisbon stock exchange at all — not its merger into Euronext in 2002, not the PSI, not a single listing — so there is no Portuguese record for a European exchange to be joined to. It is also the only one of the 42 that is not a Portuguese body.
- `altice-portugal` — Retracted in M41b: the largest Portuguese telecommunications provider, founded in 1994 and a wholly owned subsidiary of Altice since June 2015. The atlas holds no record of the privatisation of Portugal Telecom, of the liberalisation of the fixed market in 2000, or of the sale to Altice, which are the three events this company would be joined by. Nothing here can be argued to follow from it or to have produced it.
- `meo` — Retracted in M41b: the fixed and mobile brand of Altice Portugal, which became the group's operating company when Portugal Telecom wound up TMN at the end of 2014. It is a brand and a subsidiary inside a company the atlas has already retracted for want of anything to join it to, and it would need everything Altice Portugal would need first.
- `nos` — Retracted in M41b: the telecommunications and media group formed in 2013 by the merger of Zon Multimédia with Sonae's Optimus. The atlas holds neither company, neither the cable television of the 1990s nor the mobile licences, so a merger of two records it does not have cannot be argued to follow from anything here.
- `farfetch` — Retracted in M41b: the luxury fashion marketplace founded in 2007 or 2008 by José Neves, headquartered in London and bought by the Korean company Coupang in January 2024. Its founder is Portuguese and almost nothing else about it is; the atlas holds no record of Portuguese technology companies, of the London listing or of the sale, and there is nothing here for it to change or to answer to.
- `altri` — Retracted in M41b: the pulp, forestry and cogeneration group headquartered in Porto, split off in 2005 and in steel before 2008. The atlas holds nothing about Portuguese industry after the nationalisations of 1975 and the privatisations that undid them, and the leads offer no event this company took part in.
- `semapa` — Retracted in M41b: the holding company founded in 1991 with interests in cement, pulp and paper and environmental services. The same gap as Altri's and the Navigator Company's: three records of the same industry, none of them attached to an event, and an atlas with no Portuguese industrial history between 1975 and today.
- `the-navigator-company` — Retracted in M41b: the pulp and paper company, under this name since 2001. Its cached leads are one sentence each and say only what it makes. There is no event here it took part in and nothing in the record from which one could be argued.
- `brisa-auto-estradas-de-portugal` — Retracted in M41b: the motorway concessionaire founded in 1972 by Jorge de Brito, which built and runs most of the Portuguese network. The motorway programme of the 1980s and 1990s is a real consequence of accession to the Communities and would make a good record; the atlas does not hold it, and without it the company has nothing to be joined to.
- `fertagus` — Retracted in M41b: the suburban railway that has run across the Ponte 25 de Abril to the Setúbal peninsula under concession since 1999. The atlas holds no record of the bridge, of its railway deck opened in that year, or of the Lisbon suburban network, and the operator cannot be joined to a crossing the atlas has never mentioned.

## Institutions

- `university-of-porto` — Retracted in M41b: the university founded on 22 March 1911, the second largest in the country. The Republic created the universities of Lisbon and Porto by decree in its first year, and that is a real consequence of the records this atlas already holds — the proclamation of October 1910, the constitution and the law of separation of 1911. But the atlas has no record of the decree, and an actor cannot be joined to an event nobody has written.

## Parties and movements

- `national-syndicalists` — Retracted in M41b: the National Syndicalist Movement, founded in February 1932 by Francisco Rolão Preto, the nearest thing to a fascist movement Portugal produced and suppressed by the Estado Novo in 1934. The atlas holds the regime it was absorbed and banned by, and holds nothing about the suppression itself: not the ban, not the exile of Rolão Preto, not the revolt of 1935, and no record of the man. It would need one of those, and it is the retraction here most worth undoing.
- `azores-liberation-front` — Retracted in M41b: the FLA, the right-wing Azorean separatist organisation that appeared immediately after the Carnation Revolution and attacked political opponents in the islands in 1975. The atlas holds the revolution and the first Azorean regional election of 1976, and its record of that election speaks of the archipelago's autonomist movement without naming this one. It would need a record of the Azorean agitation of the summer of 1975, which is the event the FLA belongs to.
- `portuguese-workers-communist-party-re-organized-movement-of-the-party-of-the-proletariat` — Retracted in M41b: the PCTP/MRPP, the Maoist party founded on 18 September 1970 and legalised in 1975. The atlas holds the revolutionary period in some detail and no record of the far left outside the PCP; the leads say when the party was founded and renamed and nothing about anything it did. There is no honest way from either to the other.
- `livre` — Retracted in M41b: LIVRE, the green and pro-European left party legalised in March 2014. It has held seats in the Assembly since 2019 and its cached leads name no election at all, only its founding principles; no record in this atlas names it either. It would be joined by a legislative election record that named it, exactly as Iniciativa Liberal was.
- `volt-portugal` — Retracted in M41b: the Portuguese chapter of Volt Europa, registered in June 2020 as the twenty-fifth party in the country. It has contested elections and won no seat, the leads name no result, and nothing in the atlas names it. There is nothing to argue from.
- `social-liberal-party` — Retracted in M41b: the Partido Liberal Social, accepted by the Constitutional Court on 11 March 2025. It is two months older than the most recent record in this atlas and has done nothing yet that any source describes. Retracted for want of anything at all to say, not for want of importance.
- `rise-up` — Retracted in M41b: Ergue-te, the ultranationalist party founded in 2000 as the Partido Nacional Renovador out of a statute change to the old Partido Renovador Democrático, and dissolved in 2025. It never held a seat, the atlas holds neither the PRD nor any election record that names it, and the leads describe a motto rather than an event.
- `new-macau-association` — Retracted in M41b: the pro-democracy association founded in Macau in 1992 by António Ng Kuok Cheong, which won seats in the Legislative Assembly from 2005. The atlas's two Macau records are the chief executive selection of May 1999 and the transfer of administration that December, and this association took part in neither; everything the leads describe about it happened under Chinese sovereignty, after the Portuguese period this atlas covers ends.
- `partido-nacionalista-portugal` — Retracted in M41b: the Nationalist Party founded in 1903 by Jacinto Cândido after his break with Hintze Ribeiro. It is the only one of the 42 with no English lead, and the single Portuguese sentence cached for it says who founded it and nothing more. The atlas holds the election of 1908 in which both parties of the rotation had already split, but names only the Regenerators; joining this party to it would need a source that says how it stood, and there is no record here of Jacinto Cândido.

financial crisis — `caused` the request for assistance of April 2011
